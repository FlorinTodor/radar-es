Incidents and emergencies in Spain, live

The Radar España map brings together traffic incidents, weather warnings, official civil-protection emergencies and power cuts, wildfires, earthquakes and the state of the power grid, from the official sources that publish them. They are in the Incidencias tab, with filters by type, and each one can be seen on the map.

Traffic

Accidents, roadworks, queues, closures and other road incidents. The DGT, Spain's traffic authority, publishes those for almost all of Spain; Catalonia and the Basque Country have their own traffic authorities, so incidents from the Catalan Traffic Service and from Open Data Euskadi (including Bilbao, Vitoria-Gasteiz and Donostia/San Sebastián) are added.

Each incident leaves the map when its source stops publishing it. Most DGT roadworks come with their expected end: it is shown on their card and the roadworks disappear when it arrives, even if the DGT still publishes them. If a traffic source has not answered for an hour, its incidents stop being shown: without news from it, there is no way to know whether they are still there.

Weather warnings

AEMET warnings for rain, wind, snow, heat, storms or coastal events, with their level (yellow, orange or red), the affected area and the period they are in force. If trains are running inside an area with a serious warning, the alert says so.

Wildfires

  • Hotspots detected by NASA satellites (FIRMS). A hotspot is a possible fire, not a confirmation: it can also be agricultural burning or an industrial site.
  • Burnt areas mapped by the European Forest Fire Information System (Copernicus EFFIS), as a semi-transparent layer.
  • If there is a hotspot a few kilometres from running trains, an alert appears.

Earthquakes

Earthquakes from the National Geographic Institute (IGN) in and around Spain from magnitude 1.5 (small ones too, such as earthquake swarms), with their magnitude, time and the place the IGN gives them. Those the IGN does not locate, mostly in France, Portugal and Morocco, come from the EMSC-CSEM SeismicPortal. They are preliminary data that are reviewed later. Those from the last two days are shown. Those above a certain magnitude raise an alert that says how many trains are running nearby.

International disasters

Alerts from the Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System (GDACS) that affect Spain: floods, cyclones, earthquakes or volcanic eruptions, as in the Canary Islands.

Power grid and blackouts

Peninsular electricity demand from Red Eléctrica, compared with its forecast. If it suddenly falls well below the forecast, Radar España raises an alert of a possible blackout or major outage. It is our own estimate from public data, not a warning from Red Eléctrica.

Demand on each island (or group of islands) of the Canaries, Ceuta and Melilla is watched too: if it falls to almost zero or far below what is expected, an alert of a possible blackout appears within minutes, usually before the official notice is published.

Official emergency notices and power cuts

Civil protection plans in force (pre-alert, alert or emergency) and power cuts published by the Government of the Canary Islands (for example, an island-wide blackout), the Government of the Balearic Islands, the Generalitat de Catalunya, the Junta de Andalucía, the Castilla-La Mancha 112, SOS Rioja 112 and the Castilla y León 112. For the Community of Madrid, the Madrid City Council (for the city), the Region of Murcia, the Government of Cantabria, the Xunta de Galicia, the Basque Government and the Principality of Asturias, whose 112 services do not publish their notices as open data, their government press releases are read: only those that activate or end an emergency plan or report a power cut. Their text, or the excerpt about the power cut or the plan, is quoted unaltered, in Spanish or Catalan, with its source and date, at the centre of the island, town, province or region they name, and they disappear once their end is announced. Power cuts and plans at alert or emergency level also raise an alert.

When a power cut appears on the map

Radar España does not measure the power in each home. A power cut only appears if one of these three things happens:

  • An official body publishes it in a covered region (civil protection, its 112 or its government) and the notice says there is a power cut and where. Each source is read every 3 to 15 minutes.
  • An island of the Canaries, Ceuta or Melilla goes dark: its electricity demand (e·sios, from Red Eléctrica) falls below half of what is expected in two consecutive readings, which arrive every 5 minutes.
  • Demand across the whole peninsula falls 10 % or more below Red Eléctrica's forecast, as on 28 April 2025.

So these do not appear:

  • power cuts in a street, a neighbourhood or a town that no official body publishes: only the electricity distributors (e-distribución, i-DE, UFD…) know about them, and they do not publish them as open data;
  • power cuts lasting a few minutes: they are over before anyone publishes them or before the next reading;
  • notices from regions without a source that can be reused: Aragón, Valencian Community, Extremadura and Navarre. For Ceuta and Melilla only a total blackout shows, from their demand;
  • a total blackout of the Balearic Islands from its demand: Red Eléctrica does not publish the islands' real demand in real time (the Balearic Government's notices do show).

A power cut disappears when the official body announces that power is back or, if it does not, the next day. If a power cut does not appear, that does not mean there is none: in an emergency, call 112.

Transport notices

Renfe service notices, the status of each Rodalies de Catalunya line and EMT Madrid notices. Trains, metro and buses are explained in live trains and metro, tram and bus.

Cancelled trains and buses

Those that their operators report as cancelled or with skipped stops in their real-time data: Renfe (Cercanías, Rodalies, high-speed, long and medium distance), Euskotren, Barcelona's metropolitan buses (AMB) and, through their detour notices, the city buses of Valladolid (AUVASA) and Málaga (EMT). Each one appears at its origin station (or at the first skipped stop), under the «Cancelaciones» filter of the Incidents tab and on the card of every station it was due to call at. Its card shows which train it is, from where to where, its timetabled departure and the time at each stop, which stops are skipped, who publishes it and in which file.

The reason, when the operator gives it: if a Renfe notice names the train (by its number, or by its departure time and origin station), the card quotes it; notices about its line that may explain it (engineering works, a breakdown, a strike) are listed separately. For buses, the reason is their detour notice.

The sources do not say when they published it: the card shows when the radar first saw it (it reads them every one or two minutes) and warns if it was already published when the radar started reading them. When the operator stops publishing it, it stays on the map, faded, until midnight, with the time it was withdrawn: after its arrival time that is normal (operators remove trips that have already gone); before it, the train may run after all. Renfe does not give the day of the cancelled train, and sometimes reports it as both cancelled and running: the card says so. No cancellations appear for the other operators (Ouigo, Iryo, FGC, Metro Bilbao, the metro and tram networks and the other bus companies): either they do not publish them as open data or, like Metro Bilbao, they publish real-time trip updates but so far without any cancellation.

Trains with no data

A high-speed, long or medium distance train that, according to the timetable Renfe publishes, should be running (more than 10 minutes after its departure and more than 5 before its arrival) and about which Renfe publishes nothing in real time: no position, no delay, no cancellation. It appears in grey, with a question mark, at the last station it should have passed, and on the card of the stations on its route. It is not a cancellation: it may be running without Renfe publishing its position, or not running without its cancellation having been published. If almost all of them are missing, Renfe's position file is what is failing and none is marked.

Before you use it

Radar España is not an official website: it shows the data published by its sources, seconds to minutes late, and it does not replace civil protection or official warnings. In an emergency, call 112. Sources and licences are listed in the sources and licences page (in Spanish).
